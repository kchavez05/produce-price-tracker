## Identity and search requirements

- Preserve all 72 accepted IDs, existing labels/aliases/estimates/recipe links unless an explicit reviewed decision changes their presentation. A new analytical source ID is not a new cooking identity.
- Separate material forms and cooking-relevant varieties. Source descriptions, fingerprints, publication dates and provenance remain inspectable outside the browser bundle.
- Broad queries must stay compact and every intended primary choice must remain discoverable. The first approved batch reaches 12 results for `beans` and ten for `flour`; do not append more matching primary entries and hide them by truncation. Define and review a grouping/navigation extension before further growth.
- Retain direct queries and optional navigation for specific varieties. Search grouping never establishes coverage. The approved rice-flour varieties add no compatibility edge.
- Consider remaining mung/adzuki/lima/black-eyed-pea forms, named lentil varieties, rice forms, and flour gaps during the coverage audit. Their deferral is an open release-scope decision, not evidence that the category is complete.

## Recipe and price requirements

Every identity needs a reasoned market-applicability disposition against the exact market catalog and recipe-use rules. Do not infer links from names or attach FDC identity records to price feeds.

Existing market groups such as broccoli/cauliflower, spinach/kale, white/cremini mushrooms, tomatoes/tomatillos, cottage cheese/sour cream, and plain/vanilla Greek yogurt can cross cooking identities. Before publishing an affected link, review whether to split market identities or represent explicit recipe alternatives. Never let ownership of one ingredient establish another. Preserve observed-offer provenance and uncertainty during any market split; no historical price relabeling without evidence.

New ingredients can be explicitly unpriced. Existing fixed recipe-use estimates remain intact. New estimates, source equivalence, recipe compatibility, market mapping, and new recipe requirements require their own explicit reviewed decisions. No automatic substitutions, inventory deductions, nutritional imports, or dietary guarantees.

## Integration after each batch approval

1. Record the real reviewer/date and exact unchanged decision hashes. Retain approval history and explicit deferred items.
2. Compile a unique reviewed artifact against the last accepted parent. Replay the complete checksum-matching source snapshot, revalidate inherited decisions/quarantine scope, and check both approved and preview search fixtures.
3. Refresh the complete market applicability audit and generated runtime projection from reviewed data. Keep research/proposal files outside runtime imports.
4. Add an additive server accepted-ID migration before newer clients can queue additions; retain all older accepted IDs. Review SQLite/Postgres readiness and rollback together.
5. Verify quantity-free add/save/reload, guest isolation, authenticated session persistence, second-client access, duplicates, optional-variety navigation, stale revisions, export/deletion, account-bound retries, and unknown-ID preservation. Test recipe cost/coverage/shopping changes for each approved link, including mixed-form cases.
6. Take implementation PRs to `develop` through required local checks, CI, Vercel Preview and completed Codex review. No production migration or activation is performed by preparing a proposal.

## Release completion

Before calling the catalog expansion complete, review every batch disposition and the final coverage report. Explicitly agree any remaining deferrals and their reasons; a discovery row count is not the completion metric. Verify broad-query completeness and the full market-link audit against final content.

Run typecheck, lint, unit, smoke, regression, catalog projection/audit checks, and production build. Prepare the version bump, current README/wiki/roadmap/changelog updates, OpenAPI version/export updates where required, and detailed release notes through a PR. Follow the repository's `develop` to `main` release workflow, hosted verification, release publication, and any existing milestone closure.
