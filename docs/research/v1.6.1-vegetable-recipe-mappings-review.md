This assesses **all 22 candidate uses across nine blueprints** for the five affected market groups, including low-carb replacements. Low-carb candidate labels describe existing recipe options and do not establish dietary guarantees.

Three choices deserve particular attention:

- **Cauliflower mash:** narrow the beef braise’s misleading “Cauliflower & Broccoli Florets” label to cauliflower. Its directions make cauliflower mash, so broccoli alone will not cover it.
- **Cremini braise:** white mushrooms cannot cover its cremini/baby bella slot. White or cremini is acceptable only in the generic mushroom slots listed above. This batch adds no cremini identity.
- **Tomatoes:** tomato-only curry prices must identify tomatoes. The green chile stew explicitly accepts tomatoes or tomatillos, so either qualifying offer can price that alternative. Pantry tomatoes can cover the stew without creating a tomatillo inventory entry.

Implementation must make the directions agree with the chosen option: name broccoli or cauliflower in the chicken casserole; allow time to cook kale in the Tuscan stew; state the cauliflower replacement in the curry; describe cooking spinach/kale before adding it to the breakfast casserole or serving it as the salmon side. These latter greens choices are explicit proposals for existing grouped candidates, not claims that the current directions already explain them.

Carrots have a direct fresh recipe-use relationship to `carrots-whole-baby`; the four mixed groups require recipe/slot context. They must not be globally mapped to a single pantry identity. Custom, edited, stale or unrecognized saved recipes do not inherit an expanded alternative just because they contain a grouped market ID. Unreviewed context stays uncovered and unpriced until reviewed; matching must validate the slot context, not trust caller-supplied alternatives.

## Offer and shopping rules

Keep all five market identities and their observed history intact. Do not split a historical price into multiple specific-vegetable prices. These are conditional recipe-use relationships, not evidence that the market group and pantry food are identical.

A price is usable only when its original offer title identifies an allowed fresh food and no disallowed sibling or incompatible preparation. Whole/baby carrots and raw crowns/florets can qualify. Frozen, canned, dried, powdered, juiced, sauced, soup, dip, crust and prepared dishes do not qualify for these fresh slots. A title such as “Sliced mushrooms” has no species evidence, so it stays unpriced for both white/cremini rules. “Fresh vegetables” also stays unpriced. Named foods must be recognized as complete terms rather than substring guesses.

“Fresh Roma Tomatoes or Husks Tomatillos” is usable for the stew’s explicit alternative, but cannot price the curry’s tomato-only choice. “Fresh kale” cannot price a spinach-only slot. A white-mushroom offer cannot price the cremini braise. A rejected cheapest offer must not hide a different qualifying store offer.

Apply the same rule independently to every store offer, the recipe card, coverage, additional cost, shopping aggregation and trip optimizer. Keep different recipe-use scopes separate when aggregating needs; two slots sharing a market ID may accept different foods. Unknown prices remain visible and totals remain marked incomplete. A fallback or another store’s price must not acquire verified status for a store that has no qualifying offer. Available pantry ingredients cover their permitted requirement at zero additional cost even when no qualifying market price exists.

The [25 behavior examples and additional integration cases](../../catalog/proposals/2026-11-06-vegetable-recipe-mappings/behavior-cases.json) specify acceptance criteria. They are **expected behavior, not executed application tests**. Implementation must add executable tests across ranking, saved recipes, requirement coverage, store prices and shopping before activation. Recipe evaluation must not mutate inventory, reinterpret old grouped inventory as a specific vegetable, or accept synthetic recipe-use IDs as pantry evidence.
