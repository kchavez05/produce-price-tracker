## Important review boundaries

- **Dried pulses:** the SR descriptions say mature seeds, raw. The proposal interprets these as the dried cooking ingredient, following the initial red-lentil review pattern. Confirm that interpretation for each row. Do not confuse mung beans with mungo/black gram or sprouts.
- **Canned beans:** Foundation descriptions include sodium-added, drained/rinsed samples. These support a generic canned identity, not a rinsing requirement or a sodium/nutrition claim. Kidney beans use the generic all-types SR record rather than relabeling a red variety as every kidney bean.
- **Flour:** enrichment and bleaching do not create separate all-purpose choices. Bread flour and self-rising flour remain separate from all-purpose. Besan/gram flour aliases follow the selected SR description; no alias establishes ownership of a different pulse flour.
- **Rice flour:** Brown rice flour and Glutinous rice flour are optional children of the existing Rice flour identity. Broad `rice` still returns the original three dry-rice choices. Broad `flour` shows the existing Rice flour parent, and specific variety queries/optional choices reveal the children. Varieties inherit the parent’s flour-qualified discovery restriction: `brown rice` and `glutinous rice` do not show flour choices. No new recipe compatibility is proposed, including child-to-parent coverage. “Glutinous” is not a dietary certification.
- **Grains:** rolled/steel-cut oats and hulled/pearled barley remain separate. Whole buckwheat is not roasted kasha; millet and sorghum grains are not their flours. Dry pasta is an unspecified generic choice and does not establish a named pasta type, whole-wheat variety, or dietary property.
- **Existing rice/flour identities:** do not attach a specific long-grain or white-rice source to a general inherited identity merely to increase provenance counts. Basmati/jasmine/brown rice, generic rice flour, masa harina, cannellini, and Great Northern beans still need targeted provenance review.
